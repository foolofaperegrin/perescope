Oct 2024 · Wānaka Arts Society

Wānaka Arts Society — artist in residence

In October 2024 I spent two weeks on the shores of beautiful lake Wānaka as the Artist in Residence for the Wānaka Arts Society's 45th annual Labour Weekend Exhibition.

I stayed in a cosy cottage with a view of snowcapped mountains, and spent my days focusing on creating and developing my practice for the exhibition at the residency's conclusion. The president of the society also provided me with a mountain bike, and my gracious hosts permitted use of their Land Cruiser, so I could explore the region — surely one of Aotearoa's most scenic.

The first week I settled in and worked through the challenge of imaging a wide variety of subjects for exhibiting. A trip to Diamond Lake Conservation Area turned up striking lichens and a beautiful silk structure which came together in a micro still life, Among the Lichens (2024).

I also took water samples from the lake and found a neat little ostracod — a microscopic freshwater crustacean that protects its body with a bivalve-esque shell.

I enjoyed a wonderful bike ride around the peninsula to the inlet of the mighty Mata-Au / Clutha River. The turquoise colour of the river inspired the Clutha Eddies Series (2024), prints made from imaging inks and dyes in open reaction.

Optical illusions have interested me since childhood visits to Puzzling World. In my explorations of microscope photography I've often come across strange tricks of light — like finding a reflection of myself in tiny microscopic bubbles. I spent the last few nights of the first week using goose-necked crocodile clips to carefully place different light sources, lenses, objects and mirrors in three-dimensional space around the microscope, trained on a butchered camera lens. That lens reflected tiny images of the objects placed around it — composition is notoriously difficult in microscope imagery, but working with microscopic reflections gave me much more room to manoeuvre. The result is the Microreflections Series (2024).

That weekend I was treated by my hosts, the Johnstones, to a jetboating adventure on the West Coast. The final work I produced during the residency was a mihi to this most beautiful region — Te Tai Poutini, a visage of its dramatic landscape captured in a microscope image of its most precious substance, pounamu: Te Tai Poutini (2024).

The following week was busy with creative workshops — two at Mt Aspiring College, where art students used the microscope cameras to photograph their own subjects — and the opening of the Labour Weekend Art Exhibition at Lake Wānaka Centre. More than 140 artists exhibited over the weekend, with more than 400 pieces of art.

Beneath my works, printed and mounted by PrintArt, I was thrilled to let the audience get hands-on with one of the microscopes and a large screen so they could explore the miniscule world around us.

I'm really thrilled to have been selected as the WAS Artist in Residence, especially given how left-of-field my work is. I deeply appreciate the support and positive feedback on my art and workshops during my time there — and the hospitality of Rob and Jean Johnstone.
